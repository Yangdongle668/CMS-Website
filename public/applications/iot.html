IoT Device Battery

Deployed once, replaced rarely. The battery is the uptime.

The Reality

An IoT node is only as reliable as its cell.

Half of IoT field failures trace back to battery behaviour under edge conditions — cold storage rooms, rooftop heat, high-humidity shipping containers, decade-long buried asset tags. We build cells sized for the environment, not the datasheet, and we de-rate the duty cycle calculation for the actual deployment temperature distribution rather than the convenient 25 °C number.

Chemistry decision tree

Two architectures cover 95% of IoT deployments.

Most IoT battery selection mistakes come from picking the wrong chemistry for the duty cycle. The split is binary: low-event vs high-event devices.

Low-event: water meter, smart agriculture, asset tag.

Devices that wake once per hour or less, draw 50 to 150 µA average, and need 10+ year passive life. Ultra-low self-discharge Li-SOCl₂ (< 1% per year, < 0.5% with passivation) is the only chemistry that mathematically supports the target. Capacity: 1.2 Ah (½AA) to 19 Ah (DD).

For LoRa, NB-IoT or LTE-M radios, add a hybrid pulse capacitor (HPC) sized for the worst-case transmit burst. The HPC sources the 1 to 4 A pulse; the Li-SOCl₂ recharges the HPC between transmissions. Net: 10-year life with cellular-radio capability. Read our cell-sizing from a power profile guide for the worked LoRa example.

High-event: cold-chain logger, PoC tracker, smart lock.

Devices that wake every few minutes or constantly stream data — too high a duty cycle for primary chemistry to last 10 years cost-effectively. Use rechargeable LiFePO4 with solar harvesting (10+ year life, 3,000+ cycles) for outdoor deployments, or replaceable Li-MnO₂ coin/CR-series cells (1 to 3 year life, accept low cost) for indoor.

For sub-zero cold-chain (−40 °C continuous), Li-MnO₂ wins both on cold-temp capacity (70 to 80% at −40 °C) and pulse-current capability. Capacity: 100 mAh coin to 1.7 Ah CR123. We qualify each lot through a 30-cycle thermal cycling test before release.

Common questions

What IoT teams ask before they commit.

How do you actually get 10-year IoT battery life?

Three constraints have to line up: ultra-low self-discharge chemistry (Li-SOCl₂ at < 1% per year), low-leakage protection circuit (sub-2 µA quiescent), and a duty cycle that keeps device average draw at or below cell capacity divided by 10 years. For LoRa / NB-IoT trackers reporting hourly with 50 to 150 µA average, a 17 Ah Li-SOCl₂ D-cell hits the math exactly. See sizing a lithium cell from a power profile.

How do you support cellular-radio bursts on a low-current chemistry?

Hybrid: Li-SOCl₂ primary + hybrid pulse capacitor (HPC) sized for the burst. The HPC sources the 1 to 4 A transmit pulse; the primary recharges the HPC between transmissions. Most LTE-M and NB-IoT trackers we ship use this architecture with a 1 to 5 F supercap and a 2 to 10 ms transmit window.

What changes for cold-chain (−40 °C) deployments?

Switch from Li-SOCl₂ to Li-MnO₂. Li-SOCl₂ capacity drops to 30 to 40% at −40 °C; Li-MnO₂ holds 70 to 80%. Trade-off: shorter shelf life (5 to 7 years vs 15 to 20). De-rate the duty cycle calculation for the actual deployment temperature distribution, not the nominal 25 °C.

When is rechargeable cheaper than primary over 10 years?

Only when there's meaningful energy harvesting (typically > 100 cm² solar). LiFePO4 with solar wins on TCO above that threshold. Without harvesting, primary cells almost always beat rechargeable because the BMS adds quiescent draw and the cell ages calendar-wise even unused. See how to choose Li-Po capacity for IoT devices.

Do you support ATEX hazardous-area deployments?

Yes — ATEX Zone 2 and IECEx for industrial sensors in oil/gas and chemical sites. Requires specific cell variants (no vent membrane, hermetic seal, intrinsically-safe terminals). Lead time 16 to 22 weeks for a fully certified pack. UL Class I Div 2 ships in similar timeframe.
