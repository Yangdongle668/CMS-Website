Smart Home Battery Packs

Long-life cells for devices that have to "just work" for two years between charges.

The challenge

The customer never wants to change a battery.

Smart-home devices live behind walls, on ceilings, on outdoor doorframes — anywhere a 4 AM low-battery alert is a quality-of-life problem. We ship cells into smart locks, door/window sensors, motion + PIR detectors, leak sensors, smoke alarms, security cameras, video doorbells, robot vacuums, robot mowers, smart shades and connected appliances. Different chemistries, same design rule: the battery must be invisible to the end-user for as long as physics allows.

Long-life Li-Po pouch for high-event devices.

For smart locks, video doorbells and battery-powered cameras, custom Li-Po pouches (500 mAh – 10 Ah) deliver 12–24 months between charges. Our low-self-discharge formulation holds < 1% loss per month, vs. 2–4% for commodity LCO. Standard chemistry: 4.40V LCO with carbon-coated foil; high-voltage 4.48V variant for premium platforms where +8% energy density justifies the cycle-life trade-off.

Coin steel-shell for low-event sensors.

For window sensors, motion detectors and leak alarms — devices that fire 5–50 events per day — primary lithium coin (CR2032, CR2450) and rechargeable LIR/ML give 3–8 years of life. The hermetic steel can survives -40 °C to +85 °C with no measurable self-discharge in the first three years. We supply CR-series MnO2 chemistry for true 8+ year deployments and LIR/ML rechargeables for sensors with energy-harvesting trickle charge.

Design priorities

Six rules that make a battery invisible.

Smart-home devices fail when the battery becomes visible — when it dies early, leaks, swells, or refuses to wake the device on cold mornings. Six engineering decisions decide which one happens.

①

Ultra-low self-discharge

< 1% per month at 25 °C — twice the industry standard for low-volume LCO. Critical for devices stocked on retail shelves for 6+ months.

②

Hibernate-current matching

BMS standby current under 5 µA for primary-cell platforms, under 25 µA for rechargeable. We tune the protection IC's quiescent draw to the host MCU's deep-sleep budget.

③

Wide-temperature electrolyte

-20 °C to +60 °C operating with 75% capacity retention at -20 °C. -30 °C variant for outdoor cameras and unheated outbuildings.

④

USB-C / wireless ready

Built-in fast-charge BMS for 1.5C re-charge from USB-C PD. Optional Qi-coil interface for inductive-charge cameras and wearables.

⑤

UL 1642 + UL 2054 + CE

Cell + pack-level safety, plus UN 38.3 transport, EU Battery Regulation digital passport (2027-ready) and KC / PSE for Asia-Pacific markets.

⑥

10-year shelf-life option

Primary lithium MnO2 chemistry with hermetic steel can — used by NFPA 72 smoke alarms and EN 14604 CO detectors that legally require a 10-year sealed battery.

Common questions

What smart-home OEMs ask before they commit.

How long should a smart lock battery last?

Wi-Fi smart locks (always-listening radio) last 4–8 months on 4×AA primary lithium. Z-Wave / Zigbee locks (sleep between events) last 12–24 months. A custom Li-Po pack tuned to a 25 µA BMS standby current and 10 unlocks/day usage typically delivers 18 months — about 4× longer than commodity alkaline.

Primary or rechargeable lithium for sensors?

For low-event sensors (8 events/day or fewer), CR123A or ER14250 primary lithium gives 5–8 years on a single cell. For higher-traffic devices (PIR + radar motion sensors), a 200–500 mAh Li-Po with USB-C top-up port wins on long-term cost — especially under EU Battery Regulation 2027 rules requiring user-replaceable batteries.

What battery for video doorbells and outdoor cameras?

6,000–10,000 mAh Li-Po at 3.7V (or 2S 7.4V for higher-resolution streams). Must survive -20 °C to +60 °C, support fast 1.5C charging from solar trickle, and handle 2A peaks for H.265 encoding bursts. Self-discharge under 1%/month is critical because owners forget to recharge.

How do you handle northern winters?

Standard Li-ion loses 20–35% capacity at -20 °C and refuses to charge below 0 °C. We use low-temperature electrolyte holding 75% at -20 °C, and add a self-heating BMS for unheated outbuildings or outdoor cameras. For -30 °C deployments we move to LTO or primary lithium.

What certifications are needed?

UN 38.3 (transport, mandatory globally). UL 2054 + UL 1642 (North America). CE + UKCA + EU Battery Regulation (Europe). KC 62133 (Korea), PSE (Japan). Smart-home ecosystem certs — Matter, HomeKit MFi, Google Home — sit on the host device, but the battery test reports are part of the device file.
