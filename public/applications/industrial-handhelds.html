Industrial Handheld Battery Packs

Smart packs with SMBus, hot-swap, authentication, and a 10-hour shift you can count on.

Where we ship

Built for warehouses, terminals and the warehouse floor.

Industrial handheld batteries don't live easy lives. They get dropped on concrete, sit in -25 °C freezer aisles, run continuous Wi-Fi for 10-hour shifts, and have to authenticate against a host device that won't accept a counterfeit pack. We ship into barcode scanners, mobile computers, retail and logistics terminals, ruggedized tablets, healthcare carts, payment POS handhelds, and proprietary OEM platforms across logistics, retail, food-service and field service.

Smart packs with SMBus 1.1 telemetry.

Standard architecture: 1S2P or 2S2P 18650 / 21700 cells with a TI bq40z80 / bq40z60 or Maxim MAX17320 fuel-gauging IC. Telemetry over SMBus 1.1 exposes per-cell voltage, pack current, dual NTC temperatures, cycle count, SOH and a 32-event fault log. The host device reads cycle count and predictively retires packs at 80% SOH — before they fail during a shift.

For high-security platforms we integrate SHA-1 / SHA-256 authentication chips (TI bq26100, Maxim DS28E22) — the host issues a challenge, the pack returns a hash; counterfeit packs fail authentication and the device boots in a degraded "service required" mode.

Hot-swap design.

Mobile computer platforms (Zebra TC-class, Honeywell Dolphin, Datalogic Memor) need to swap battery mid-shift without a reboot. We design packs around a "bridge battery" — a 200 mAh Li-Po that holds the device alive for 60–90 seconds while the main pack is exchanged. Connector geometry uses sequenced pins so power, ground and SMBus come up in the right order.

For freezer-grade platforms (-30 °C operating) we ship packs with self-heating BMS that pre-warms the cells using internal coil resistance before allowing fast-charge — protecting the lithium plating threshold that destroys cycle life in cold cells.

Smart battery features

Why field IT chooses smart over dumb packs.

A smart pack costs roughly 2× a basic protected pack. Six features pay that back many times over the device lifetime.

①

SMBus 1.1 / HDQ telemetry

Per-cell V/T, pack I, cycle count, SOH, time-to-empty, full-charge capacity. The host device knows what it has.

②

SHA-1 / SHA-256 auth

Counterfeit-pack rejection at boot. Customer-supplied keys flashed under HSM control on our line.

③

Hot-swap support

Bridge-battery design with sequenced connector pins. 60–90 s swap window without device reboot.

④

Predictive retirement

Field IT reads SOH from each pack on dock. Packs hit 80% SOH retire on schedule, not after a mid-shift failure.

⑤

Cold-storage operation

Self-heating BMS for freezer aisles, low-temperature electrolyte for -30 °C, fast-charge throttle below 0 °C cell temp.

⑥

Drop + ingress

1.8 m drop on 6 sides (MIL-STD-810G), IP54 standard / IP65 / IP68 variants. Cells immobilised in a glass-fibre matrix.

Common questions

What enterprise IT and platform OEMs ask.

Why do industrial handhelds need a smart battery pack?

Three reasons: (1) hot-swap during shifts, (2) counterfeit prevention via SHA challenge-response, (3) predictive retirement at 80% SOH so warehouse IT replaces packs on schedule rather than after a mid-shift failure. The TI bq40z80 / Maxim MAX17320 family of fuel-gauges is the de facto standard.

How long should a barcode-scanner battery last per shift?

An 8-hour warehouse shift on a typical mobile computer draws 800–1,200 mAh from a 3.7V pack — covering 1,500–4,000 scans, continuous Wi-Fi and a 10–20% LCD duty cycle. We size at 1.4× the worst-case shift (so 4,500–5,500 mAh) so the pack still delivers a full shift after 800 cycles (≈ 2 years of daily use).

Can you build a replacement for an existing platform?

Yes — we reverse-engineer connector pinout, SMBus message map and mechanical interface from a sample. Auth-locked platforms (Zebra, Honeywell Dolphin, Datalogic, Panasonic Toughbook) require written authorisation from the OEM or work via a licensed service program. We do not bypass platform auth without authorisation.

What ingress and drop ratings are achievable?

Standard IP54 housing, IP65 with rubber over-mould (+4–6 g), IP68 (1m / 30min) and freezer-grade variants (-30 °C). Drop survival to 1.8 m on six sides per MIL-STD-810G / IEC 60068-2-31, with cells immobilised in a glass-fibre matrix.

Do you support proprietary auth chips?

Yes. We integrate TI bq40z, bq28z, Maxim DS28E and ST auth ICs. Customer-supplied keys are flashed under HSM control on our line, with serial-number-level traceability retained 10 years per ISO 9001.
