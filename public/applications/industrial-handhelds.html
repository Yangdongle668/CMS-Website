Industrial Handheld Batteries

Slim, sealed Li-Po packs for scanners, terminals, readers and inspection devices — where every millimeter belongs to optics, not the battery cavity.

Where we fit

The handheld market where pouch beats cylindrical.

We ship custom Li-Po pouches (1,500–8,000 mAh) and 2S smart packs into barcode scanners, mobile data terminals, RFID readers, line-of-business POS handhelds, borescopes, thermal cameras and ruggedised field tablets. Polymer wins here because the housing is IP65-sealed and every millimeter of internal volume goes to optics, screen or grip — not to the round cavity a cylindrical pack would need. For the rare devices that already mandate a cylindrical pack, we are not the right supplier.

Slim Li-Po pouch packs for screen-driven handhelds.

For barcode scanners, RFID readers, line-of-business POS handhelds and inspection cameras, our 1S / 2S Li-Po pouch packs (3.7 V or 7.4 V at 1,500 to 5,000 mAh) deliver 6 to 12 hours of duty cycle inside an IP65-sealed housing. Stacked-electrode construction (vs. wound) for drop resilience, 200+ cycle life at 1C charge / 1C discharge, and a low-impedance BMS that keeps the runtime indicator accurate from full to empty.

We size the pack against the actual shift profile: 1 decode / 6 sec for retail scanners, 1 decode / 2 sec for warehouse pickers, continuous Wi-Fi backhaul for mobile data terminals — three very different current draws.

Smart 2S packs with fuel gauge for mobile data terminals.

For 8+ hour shift devices that must show an accurate runtime indicator: our 2S Li-Po smart packs include a Texas Instruments BQ40Z80 or Maxim MAX17320 fuel-gauge IC. SMBus output delivers state-of-charge, cycle count, full-charge capacity, temperature and a host-learned capacity model. Optional SHA-256 authentication IC keeps grey-market packs out of the supply chain.

Hot-swap connector design with gold-flash contacts: under 10-second swap, no host reboot, no data loss. Standard for warehouse pickers, field-service tablets, and retail POS handhelds.

Design priorities

Six rules that make an industrial pack survive in the field.

A retail-scanner battery fails on drop count. A warehouse-picker pack fails on hot-swap cycles. A field-tablet pack fails on cold-temperature charge. Six engineering decisions cover all three failure modes.

①

Stacked-electrode construction

Stacked-electrode pouches (vs. the cheaper wound construction) survive 1.5 m drop tests onto concrete because the cell can flex with the housing instead of cracking the electrode coil.

②

SMBus fuel gauge

BQ40Z80 / MAX17320-class fuel gauge with host-learned capacity model. The runtime indicator stays accurate from year 1 to year 3, so the picker / clerk never carries a "dead at 30%" device.

③

Hot-swap geometry

Gold-flash contacts on the pack lead, spring-loaded host connector, magnetic alignment. Under 10-second swap, no host reboot, no data loss. Critical for 12+ hour warehouse shifts.

④

Authentication IC

SHA-256 challenge-response on the pack PCB (ATECC608A or BQ34Z100). Stops grey-market refurbished packs from booting on warranty devices and protects the OEM's after-sale revenue.

⑤

Wide-temperature electrolyte

-20 °C to +60 °C operating, 75% capacity retention at -20 °C. Critical for outdoor field tablets, cold-chain warehouse pickers and freezer-aisle scanners.

⑥

UN 38.3 + IEC 62133-2 + IEC 62133-1

Cell-level + pack-level safety, transport compliance, FCC + CE for radio-embedded handhelds. KC + BIS for Asian-market launches. ROHS + REACH baseline.

Common questions

What handheld OEMs ask before they commit.

What capacity for a typical barcode scanner?

1,500 to 2,500 mAh at 3.7 V for an 8-hour retail shift (1 decode / 6 to 10 sec). Long-range 2D with always-on aimer + Wi-Fi: 2,500 to 4,000 mAh. Rugged hot-swap warehouse pickers: 2S 7.4 V at 3,000 to 5,000 mAh covering a 12-hour shift with one swap.

Why polymer instead of 18650 cylindrical?

IP65 housings have no spare volume for a round cavity. Pouch cells survive drop tests because they flex with the housing. Smart hot-swap geometry is much easier to build around a flat pack than a cylindrical bar.

Can you include SMBus / I²C fuel gauge?

Yes — BQ27Z561, BQ40Z80, MAX17263 or MAX17320. SoC, cycle count, FCC, temperature, host-learned capacity model. Standard for Zebra / Honeywell / Datalogic-class mobile data terminals.

Drop and ingress resistance?

Stacked-electrode pouch + shock-absorbing foam + TPU bumper. Survives 1.5 m onto concrete. IP65 ingress is delivered at the device housing level; pack contacts use gold-flash leads to resist humid retail / outdoor environments.

How do you prevent counterfeit packs?

SHA-256 authentication IC (ATECC608A or BQ34Z100). The host challenges the pack with a 128-bit nonce on every wake; only a pack signed with the OEM's secret key responds correctly. Stops grey-market refurbished packs from booting on warranty devices.

Certifications?

UN 38.3 (transport), IEC 62133-2:2017 + Amendment 1 (cell), IEC 62133-1 (pack), CE + EU Battery Regulation, FCC at device level, KC 62133 (Korea), BIS (India). Total stack: USD 30 to 50k, 16 to 20 weeks.
